# DietSense Deep Research References

## How to use this bibliography

This is the deduplicated bibliography for `five-core-mindsets.md`, `three-professional-controversies.md`, and `ten-deep-questions.md`. Numbered citations refer to this list. Peer-reviewed papers are linked by DOI and, where available, PubMed/PMC. Government guidance and databases are linked to official pages. The bibliography records what each source supports and key boundaries; citations do not imply that a source validates DietSense.

## Peer-reviewed evidence and technical research

1. Hall, K. D., Sacks, G., Chandramohan, D., Chow, C. C., Wang, Y. C., Gortmaker, S. L., & Swinburn, B. A. (2011). “Quantification of the effect of energy imbalance on bodyweight.” *The Lancet*, 378(9793), 826–837. [https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(11)60812-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(11)60812-X) | [Open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3880593/). Dynamic model of energy imbalance and bodyweight; it does not support a fixed linear kcal-to-weight rule or identify an individual's cause from one day's data.

2. Amoutzopoulos, B., Page, P., Roberts, C., Roe, M., Cade, J., Steer, T., Baker, R., Hawes, T., Galloway, C., Yu, D., & Almiron-Roig, E. (2020). “Portion size estimation in dietary assessment: a systematic review of existing tools, their strengths and limitations.” *Nutrition Reviews*, 78(11), 885–900. [https://doi.org/10.1093/nutrit/nuz107](https://doi.org/10.1093/nutrit/nuz107) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31999347/). Reviews portion-size aids and their validation; findings vary by tool and study.

3. Ho, D. K. N., Tseng, S.-H., Wu, M.-C., Shih, C.-K., Atika, A. P., Chen, Y.-C., & Chang, J.-S. (2020). “Validity of image-based dietary assessment methods: A systematic review and meta-analysis.” *Clinical Nutrition*, 39(10), 2945–2959. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2020.08.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2020.08.002) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32839035/). Included 13 studies and 606 participants; reports heterogeneous validity and energy under-reporting versus reference methods. It does not establish that every image system is inaccurate to the same degree.

4. Mifflin, M. D., St Jeor, S. T., Hill, L. A., Scott, B. J., Daugherty, S. A., & Koh, Y. O. (1990). “A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy individuals.” *The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, 51(2), 241–247. [https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/51.2.241](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/51.2.241) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2305711/). Equation derived from 498 healthy people aged 19–78; it predicts REE and is not an individual measurement or a universal clinical formula.

5. O’Neill, J. E. R., Corish, C. A., & Horner, K. (2023). “Accuracy of Resting Metabolic Rate Prediction Equations in Athletes: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis.” *Sports Medicine*, 53(12), 2373–2398. [https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-023-01896-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-023-01896-z) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37632665/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10687135/). Synthesizes athlete-specific predictive-equation performance and heterogeneity; athlete findings should not be generalized to all adults.

6. Cioffi, I., Marra, M., Pasanisi, F., & Scalfi, L. (2021). “Prediction of resting energy expenditure in healthy older adults: A systematic review.” *Clinical Nutrition*, 40(5), 3094–3103. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2020.11.027](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2020.11.027) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33288302/). Reviews REE equation accuracy in healthy older adults; no equation was consistently precise for individuals.

7. Zeevi, D., Korem, T., Zmora, N., Israeli, D., Rothschild, D., Weinberger, A., et al. (2015). “Personalized Nutrition by Prediction of Glycemic Responses.” *Cell*, 163(5), 1079–1094. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2015.11.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2015.11.001) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26590418/). Studied postprandial glucose response and a personalized intervention; it does not validate personalized calorie targets or DietSense.

8. Gardner, C. D., Trepanowski, J. F., Del Gobbo, L. C., Hauser, M. E., Rigdon, J., Ioannidis, J. P. A., Desai, M., & King, A. C. (2018). “Effect of Low-Fat vs Low-Carbohydrate Diet on 12-Month Weight Loss in Overweight Adults and the Association With Genotype Pattern or Insulin Secretion: The DIETFITS Randomized Clinical Trial.” *JAMA*, 319(7), 667–679. [https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.0245](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.0245) | [JAMA article](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2673150). Among 609 generally healthy adults without diabetes, the trial found no significant between-group weight difference and no interaction for its prespecified genotype/insulin measures; this is not evidence against every form of personalization.

9. Hall, K. D., Ayuketah, A., Brychta, R., Cai, H., Cassimatis, T., Chen, K. Y., et al. (2019). “Ultra-Processed Diets Cause Excess Calorie Intake and Weight Gain: An Inpatient Randomized Controlled Trial of Ad Libitum Food Intake.” *Cell Metabolism*, 30(1), 67–77.e3. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2019.05.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2019.05.008) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7946062/) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31269427/). Twenty adults received two-week diet periods in an inpatient crossover study. The controlled setting and short duration limit external generalization; findings do not show that food quality can be reduced to a single score.

10. Hill, D., Conner, M., Clancy, F., Moss, R., Wilding, S., Bristow, M., & O’Connor, D. B. (2022). “Stress and eating behaviours in healthy adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis.” *Health Psychology Review*, 16(2), 280–304. [https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2021.1923406](https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2021.1923406) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33913377/). Included 54 studies and 119,820 participants; observed small associations. It does not justify inferring an individual's emotional state from a meal or time of day.

11. Raber, M., Liao, Y., Rara, A., Schembre, S. M., Krause, K. J., Strong, L., Daniel-MacDougall, C., & Basen-Engquist, K. (2021). “A systematic review of the use of dietary self-monitoring in behavioural weight loss interventions: delivery, intensity and effectiveness.” *Public Health Nutrition*, 24(17), 5885–5913. [https://doi.org/10.1017/S136898002100358X](https://doi.org/10.1017/S136898002100358X) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34412727/) | [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8928602/). Reviews 59 adult overweight/obesity intervention studies. Monitoring may support weight loss, while inconsistent adherence measures and limited analysis of usage/outcome relationships constrain conclusions.

12. Roth, A. M., Gruber, J. R., Reif, A., Schultze, M., & Matura, S. (2024). “Exploring weight-related self-monitoring as a potential risk factor for eating disorder symptoms in adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis.” *Appetite*, 202, 107610. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.appet.2024.107610](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.appet.2024.107610) | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39069082/). The pooled association with global eating-disorder symptom scores was not statistically significant; individual factors may matter. This concerns weight-related self-monitoring and does not establish that all tracking is harmless or harmful.

13. Anderberg, I., Kemps, E., & Prichard, I. (2025). “The link between the use of diet and fitness monitoring apps, body image and disordered eating symptomology: A systematic review.” *Body Image*, 52, 101836. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bodyim.2024.101836](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bodyim.2024.101836). The 38-study review found associations, but most evidence was cross-sectional and cannot establish causal direction.

14. Lewis, P., Perez, E., Piktus, A., Petroni, F., Karpukhin, V., Goyal, N., et al. (2020). “Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks.” *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems*, 33, 9459–9474. [NeurIPS paper page](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/6b493230205f780e1bc26945df7481e5-Abstract.html) | [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401). Evaluates RAG on knowledge-intensive NLP tasks; it does not establish accuracy in nutrition or eliminate retrieval/generation errors.

## Official guidance and data sources

15. Taiwan Ministry of Health and Welfare, Health Promotion Administration. *國人膳食營養素參考攝取量（Dietary Reference Intakes, DRIs）第八版*, current official materials and revision background. [Official HPA DRIs page](https://www.hpa.gov.tw/Pages/Detail.aspx?feature=featurea13&kv=default&nodeid=4248&pid=12285) | [Official HPA summary table PDF](https://www.ntshb.gov.tw/df_ufiles/029/%E3%80%8C%E5%9C%8B%E4%BA%BA%E8%86%B3%E9%A3%9F%E7%87%9F%E9%A4%8A%E7%B4%A0%E5%8F%83%E8%80%83%E6%94%9D%E5%8F%96%E9%87%8F%E5%8F%8A%E5%85%B6%E8%AA%AA%E6%98%8E%E3%80%8D%E7%AC%AC%E5%85%AB%E7%89%88.pdf). Government dietary reference source for Taiwanese populations; follow the source's population-specific scope and consult professionals for special needs.

16. Institute of Medicine (US), Food and Nutrition Board. *Dietary Reference Intakes for Energy, Carbohydrate, Fiber, Fat, Fatty Acids, Cholesterol, Protein, and Amino Acids*. Washington, DC: National Academies Press, 2005. [https://doi.org/10.17226/10490](https://doi.org/10.17226/10490). Source of North American DRI/AMDR reference ranges; not a substitute for current Taiwan guidance.

17. UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE). *Eating disorders: recognition and treatment*, guideline NG69, published 2017, updated 2020. [Official recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng69/chapter/Recommendations). Supports referral and clinical-care boundaries; it is not an automated screening algorithm for DietSense.

18. Taiwan Ministry of Health and Welfare, Food and Drug Administration. *台灣地區食品營養成分資料庫* (official database page; access and data version must be checked at implementation). [Official TFDA database listing](https://www.fda.gov.tw/tc/site.aspx?r=1647620244&sid=271) | [TFDA report, 臺灣食品成分資料庫之發展現況與應用](https://www.fda.gov.tw/tc/includes/GetFile.ashx?id=f637744876252406610). The database's existence does not mean it covers all dishes, vendors, recipes, or current formulations.

19. Cunningham, J. J. (1991). “Body composition as a determinant of energy expenditure: a synthetic review and a proposed general prediction equation.” *The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, 54(6), 963–969. [https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/54.6.963](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/54.6.963) | [Journal article](https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165%2823%2931936-1/fulltext). Proposes REE = 370 + 21.6 × fat-free mass; this is the primary source for the Cunningham equation. Because secondary sources sometimes label related FFM equations differently, DietSense should identify the exact equation and source rather than rely on a formula nickname.
